# Blog SEO and AI-search authoring instructions

## Status and scope

This is the active working guide for blog-template design, drafting, and review. It applies to long-form blog posts only. Social-media templates remain deferred.

The objective is people-first, evidence-led content that is eligible for normal Google Search and Google AI-feature links. It is not to manipulate AI systems or promise placement in an AI Overview.

## Required inputs before drafting

- Target audience and their concrete problem or question.
- Search intent and expected reader outcome.
- Product/site context, if an internal link or product example is relevant.
- Credible primary sources, first-hand experience, original data, testing, or examples.
- Named author and, when relevant, factual reviewer.

If the agent lacks factual support for a claim, it must either research it, label it as an assumption, or omit it.

## Required article flow

1. Create one unique, descriptive `H1`.
2. State a direct, useful answer or key takeaway near the beginning.
3. Organize the body with question-led `H2` sections based on genuine reader needs.
4. Answer each heading before adding depth, context, or examples.
5. Add original value: experience, testing, examples, data, screenshots, or expert explanation.
6. Cite or link reliable sources for material factual claims.
7. Include practical application guidance.
8. Add descriptive internal links only where they genuinely help the reader.
9. Add an FAQ only for unanswered, high-value follow-up questions.
10. Include author, review, and meaningful update information in the final publishing metadata when available.

## Content quality checks

- Does the article solve the stated reader problem without forcing the reader to search again for the essential answer?
- Is the contribution more useful than a generic summary of existing sources?
- Are factual claims accurate, current, and visibly supported?
- Does the article make its author, expertise, limits, and uncertainty clear where relevant?
- Are the title, headings, and links descriptive rather than stuffed with repeated keywords?
- Is all important content available as ordinary text in the rendered page?

## Hard constraints

- Preserve one main title and exactly ten tags.
- Treat configured word and section ranges as editorial targets, not evidence of SEO quality by themselves.
- Do not invent facts, citations, data, testimonials, quotations, product capabilities, or author credentials.
- Do not add thin FAQ sections, keyword repetitions, `llms.txt`, AI-only markup, or artificial content chunks to influence Google AI features.
- Do not use `FAQPage` or `HowTo` structured data as an expected traffic tactic.
- Do not claim that a post will rank, receive an AI Overview citation, or obtain a Google backlink.

## Publishing handoff

Recommend, but do not implement unless explicitly asked:

- concise HTML title and meta description;
- semantic heading hierarchy and one prominent H1;
- accurate Article structured data matching visible text;
- author URL/profile and relevant images;
- crawlability, indexability, and meaningful internal links.
